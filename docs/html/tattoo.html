Tattoo

For other terms relating to tattoo, see tattoo (disambiguation).
A tattoo is a form of body modification in which ink or dye is inserted in the dermis (second layer of the skin). Tattoos are either intended to be temporary or permanent, with the distinction informing the method. Tattoos are generally placed on the body by a tattoo artist, with the process known as tattooing. Tattoos are diverse in appearance, including simple patterns, text, symbols, drawings, artworks, people, and characters. In turn, tattoos may serve a religous, symbolic, cosmetic, medical, or identifying purpose. The earliest physical appearance of tattoos are on the natural mummy Ötzi (3370--3100 BC), comprised of many hand-tapped lines. While tattoos are most often applied to humans, other animals may also have tattoos for identification or decoration.
